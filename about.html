The principal

Brian C. Keegan, Ph.D.

Brian is a computational social scientist who has spent his career studying how people, data, and algorithms shape one another — in online communities, in markets, in crises, and in the institutions trying to govern all three.

His doctoral and postdoctoral training spans MIT, Northwestern, Northeastern, and Harvard Business School, and he is on the faculty of Information Science at the University of Colorado Boulder. His research has examined large-scale collaboration on Wikipedia, the dynamics of breaking-news and high-tempo events, social network analysis, and the practice of auditing data and algorithms for the errors that hide in plain sight.

That academic grounding is the point. The questions clients bring — Is this model trustworthy? Did this intervention work? What does the evidence actually support? — are the same questions peer review exists to answer. Anticipation Consulting brings that level of scrutiny to decisions that can't wait for a journal's timeline.

Brian works in the open whenever he can: reproducible code, public data, and methods that others can inspect and re-run. His view is that an analyst's job is not to give a confident answer, but an honest one.

How I work

Four commitments on every engagement.

01

Honesty over confidence

If the data can't answer your question, you'll hear that first — before you spend on an analysis that can't deliver.

02

Reproducible by default

Every result ships with the code and documentation to re-derive it. No black boxes, no “trust me.”

03

Documented for scrutiny

Work is written up as if an opposing expert will read it — because sometimes one will.

04

Independent judgment

No platform to sell and no product to push. The only thing on offer is the analysis.
