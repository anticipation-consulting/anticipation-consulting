Where deep domain knowledge matters most.

Good methods are necessary but not sufficient. The hard part is knowing how a system behaves — how communities form, how information moves under pressure, where data quietly lies. Two decades of research is what makes it possible to anticipate that for clients today.

Research foundations

Seven threads, one practice.

These are the questions a career of peer-reviewed research has been spent on. They're also the lenses every client engagement is seen through.

01 Online communities & peer production

How large, loosely-coordinated groups build knowledge and value together — Wikipedia, open source, forums, platforms — and where that coordination strains or fails.

02 High-tempo & crisis events

How people, information, and institutions behave when events move faster than anyone can plan for — breaking news, emergencies, viral moments.

03 Social network analysis

Structure, influence, community, and flow in social and information networks — including methods for separating meaningful ties from noise.

04 Data & algorithm auditing

Exploratory data auditing: systematic ways to surface the biases, leaks, and artifacts that compromise an analysis before they compromise a decision.

05 Public-interest data & values

Elections, government records, and open data — and the conviction that data work carries public consequences that deserve to be taken seriously.

06 Applied demographics

Population structure, segmentation, and change — who a community, market, or region is made of, and how that composition is shifting.

07 Forecasting & projection

Projecting participation, demand, and population change from historical trends — with the uncertainty stated as plainly as the estimate.

Research is not a credential here. It's the working method.
